# Aircraft Exterior Detailing at Teterboro, Morristown, Allentown & White Plains

The exterior of your aircraft accumulates jet exhaust residue, deicing fluid, hydraulic bleed, bug and bird strikes, and airborne pollutants at every flight cycle. Left untreated, that contamination oxidizes into the clear coat, dulls the finish, and eventually requires paint correction to reverse. `{{custom_values.business_name}}` handles the full exterior appearance cycle, from routine trip cleans to full cut and polish work that restores neglected paint to showroom condition.

## What Is Aircraft Exterior Detailing?

Aircraft exterior detailing covers the appearance care work on the aircraft's outer surfaces: fuselage, wings, empennage, engine cowlings, and belly. It ranges from quick wash-and-wipe services performed between charter legs to labor-intensive multi-day polish work that removes years of oxidation. Because pressure washing is generally avoided on aircraft (weight, corrosion, avionics exposure), most exterior work uses dry wash chemistry and specialized techniques rather than water.

## Our Exterior Detailing Process

Step one: **Inspection.** Our team walks the aircraft to assess paint condition, existing damage, and contamination level, then confirms scope.

Step two: **Surface prep.** For dry wash: full aircraft dry wash using aviation-approved chemistry, panel by panel. For polish work: aircraft receives full chemical and iron decontamination first.

Step three: **Polish work (if applicable).** Machine polish is applied panel by panel to bring back clarity and remove defects.

Step four: **Protection.** If sealant or ceramic coating was ordered, it is applied after polish work is complete. If sealant only, curing takes minutes to hours.

Step five: **Inspection and documentation.** Post-service walk with your pilot or ground crew, plus written service record with any observations for future reference.

## Why Aircraft Exterior Detailing Matters

**Paint preservation.** Regular exterior work removes contaminants before they bond permanently to the clear coat.

**UV protection.** Sealants and coatings shield the paint from the UV radiation responsible for oxidation.

**Chemical protection.** Aircraft face constant exposure to hydraulic fluid, deicing chemistry, brake dust, and jet exhaust. Protective layers reduce staining.

**Cleaning efficiency.** Well-maintained paint cleans up 30 to 50 percent faster during routine trip cleans.

**Resale value.** Exterior condition and documented service history affect aircraft valuation directly. A repaint on a mid-size jet costs $75,000 or more.

## Frequently Asked Questions

#### What is a dry wash and why do you use it?

Dry wash uses aviation-approved chemistry applied and buffed panel by panel to lift contaminants without water. It avoids the corrosion, weight, and avionics exposure risks associated with wet washing aircraft, and it delivers a comparable finish. Most exterior work in private aviation uses dry wash rather than pressure washing.

#### How often should my aircraft receive exterior detail?

Depends on flight hours, hangar conditions, and storage environment. Aircraft based outdoors or with heavy flight cycles benefit from quarterly dry wash and annual decontamination and polish. Hangared aircraft with lower cycles can extend intervals.

#### Can polish work be done outdoors?

We strongly prefer to perform polish and coating work indoors in a controlled environment. Outdoor polish is possible for smaller aircraft in the right weather window, but interior application of coatings requires shelter.

#### What is the difference between 1 Step Polish and Cut & Polish?

One Step Polish is a single-stage machine polish appropriate for aircraft with light oxidation or paint that needs a refresh. Cut and Polish is a multi-stage correction for aircraft with heavier oxidation, water spotting, or years of neglect. Cut and Polish takes significantly more time and product.

#### Do I need decontamination if my aircraft is hangared?

Hangared aircraft accumulate less airborne fallout but still pick up jet exhaust residue and hydraulic bleed during flight. Annual decontamination is recommended for most aircraft, hangared or not.
