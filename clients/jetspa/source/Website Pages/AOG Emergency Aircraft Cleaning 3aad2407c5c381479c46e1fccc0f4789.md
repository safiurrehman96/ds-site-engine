## What Is AOG Aircraft Cleaning?

AOG stands for Aircraft on Ground, aviation industry terminology for an aircraft that cannot be released for its next flight until a specific problem is resolved. In the appearance care world, AOG usually means a cabin situation that must be cleaned before passengers board again: passenger illness, food spill, biohazard, chemical contamination, or any incident that would delay departure. Speed is the product.

`{{custom_values.business_name}}` offers AOG response across the Northeast, including New Jersey, New York, Pennsylvania, Connecticut, Maryland, Delaware, and Virginia. Response times depend on distance from our primary catchment at Teterboro, Morristown, Lehigh Valley, and White Plains. Standard AOG fees range from $500 to $2,000 depending on aircraft class and required response window.

Bookings with 24+ hours of notice do not qualify as AOG and are billed at our standard service rates. AOG applies specifically when the situation requires same-day or overnight response.

## Our AOG Response Process

Step one: **Emergency intake.** You call our AOG line with your aircraft location, aircraft type, issue description, and departure deadline. Intake takes minutes.

Step two: **Dispatch.** We confirm ETA and dispatch the closest available team with all containment and cleaning materials.

Step three: **On-site containment.** First priority is safe containment of the affected area, especially for biohazard incidents. PPE and containment protocols are followed without exception.

Step four: **Deep clean and treatment.** The affected area receives a full deep clean, odor treatment as required, and disinfection of surrounding surfaces.

Step five: **Documentation.** Written service record noting the incident, actions taken, materials used, and any recommended follow-up. This documentation supports insurance claims and operator maintenance logs.

## Why AOG Response Matters

**Departure protection.** A cabin incident that would delay a scheduled charter can cost the operator thousands in refunds, rebooking, and reputation damage.

**Passenger experience.** Boarding a cabin that still shows or smells the previous incident is unacceptable at any tier of private aviation.

**Biohazard safety.** Certain cabin incidents require certified handling to protect passengers, crew, and future service personnel. Untrained handling creates its own liability.

**Insurance documentation.** Written AOG service records support insurance claims and satisfy operator log requirements.

**Reputation.** Charter operators lose future bookings when passengers post about visible cabin issues online. Fast, professional response protects the brand.

#### What counts as AOG?

Any interior situation requiring cleaning before the aircraft's next scheduled departure when standard scheduling windows (24+ hours) are not available. Common cases include passenger illness, food or beverage spills, biohazard, chemical contamination, and unexpected cabin issues discovered during preflight.
