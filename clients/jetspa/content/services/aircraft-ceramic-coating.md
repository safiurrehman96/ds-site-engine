intro:
  heading: "What Is Aircraft Ceramic Coating?"
  body: |
    Aircraft ceramic coating is a semi-permanent chemical sealant that bonds to the aircraft's clear coat, creating a durable hydrophobic layer that protects against UV oxidation, chemical staining, bug and bird strikes, and hangar rash. Unlike a wax or polymer sealant that lasts weeks or months, a properly applied ceramic coating on aircraft-grade primer and paint can last from 1 year up to 5 years depending on the product warranty.

    For private aviation specifically, ceramic coating provides three practical benefits: it reduces cleaning time between flights, it preserves paint value at resale, and it protects the finish from the specific contaminants aircraft face (jet exhaust residue, deicing fluid, hydraulic fluid, brake dust).

    JetSpa applies three warranty tiers of ceramic coating:

    - **1 Year Coating (Charlie).** Entry point protection for owners who prefer to refresh annually.
    - **2 Year Coating (Bravo).** Mid-tier protection with fuller hydrophobic performance.
    - **5 Year Coating (Alfa).** Long-warranty protection for owners who want the maximum protective window.

    Coating tier selection depends on aircraft type, hangar conditions, flight hours per year, and owner preference. We help you choose the right tier during the quote conversation.

whyItMatters:
  heading: "Why Aircraft Ceramic Coating Matters"
  body: |
    Aircraft paint is one of the most expensive components on the airframe outside of the powerplant. A full repaint on a mid-size jet routinely runs $75,000 to $150,000 and takes the aircraft out of service for weeks. Ceramic coating is not about aesthetics. It is asset preservation.

    **UV protection.** Ceramic coatings block the UV radiation responsible for paint oxidation and clear coat failure over time.

    **Chemical protection.** Aircraft face constant exposure to jet exhaust, hydraulic fluid, deicing chemistry, and brake dust. Coating creates a barrier that makes contamination easier to remove and reduces staining.

    **Hydrophobic behavior.** Water beads off the surface at cruise speed. Rain, ice, and mist have less contact time with the paint, reducing weathering.

    **Cleaning time.** A coated aircraft cleans up 30 to 50 percent faster than an uncoated one during routine trip cleans and dry wash services.

    **Resale value.** Well-documented ceramic coating history is a documented service item that buyers and brokers factor into aircraft valuation.

faqs:
  - q: "How long does the aircraft need to be grounded after ceramic coating?"
    a: |
      24 to 48 hours minimum, depending on the coating chosen and ambient conditions. During this window the aircraft cannot fly, be wet-washed, or exposed to precipitation. This is non-negotiable. Flying too early causes coating adhesion failure and voids the warranty.
  - q: "Can ceramic coating be applied to an aircraft with existing paint issues?"
    a: |
      It depends. Sound paint with minor imperfections is a good candidate after polish and prep. Aircraft with active clear coat failure, delamination, or heavy oxidation may need paint correction first, or in some cases, a repaint before coating is possible. We inspect and advise during the quote process.
  - q: "Is aircraft ceramic coating approved by the manufacturer?"
    a: |
      We only use ceramic coating products approved by the original equipment manufacturer of the aircraft we are servicing. Approval status varies by airframe manufacturer and coating brand. This is confirmed during the quote conversation.
  - q: "How does the 1, 2, and 5 year warranty differ?"
    a: |
      The warranty period reflects the coating chemistry's expected durability under normal aircraft usage. Longer warranty coatings require more meticulous prep, more product, and more application time, and they cost more up front. Owners who want maximum time between coatings choose the 5 year tier; owners who prefer annual refresh cycles choose 1 or 2 year products.
