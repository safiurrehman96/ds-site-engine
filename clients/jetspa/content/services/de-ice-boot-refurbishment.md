intro:
  heading: "What Is De-Ice Boot Refurbishment?"
  body: |
    De-ice boot refurbishment is the conditioning and restoration process performed on the rubber pneumatic boots that mechanically break up ice on aircraft leading edges. Over time, exposure to UV radiation, ozone, hydraulic fluid, and airborne contaminants degrades the rubber, causing surface cracking, loss of flexibility, and reduced ice-shedding performance. Refurbishment restores flexibility, appearance, and function without requiring boot replacement.

    Boot replacement costs thousands of dollars per surface and requires significant aircraft downtime. Regular refurbishment extends boot life by years and keeps the safety system functioning as designed.

faqs:
  - q: "How long is the aircraft grounded after boot refurbishment?"
    a: |
      24 to 48 hours minimum. During this window the aircraft cannot fly. This time is required for the boot conditioner to cure and bond properly. Flying earlier causes the product to fail and the boots to look worse than before service.
  - q: "How often should de-ice boots be refurbished?"
    a: |
      Annually for most operators, or more often for aircraft based outdoors, aircraft in high UV environments, or aircraft with heavy flight cycles. Your maintenance provider may have specific intervals in your program.
  - q: "Can severely cracked or damaged boots be refurbished?"
    a: |
      No. Refurbishment restores flexibility and appearance for boots that are still structurally sound. Boots with active delamination, deep cracking through the rubber, or torn surfaces need replacement, not conditioning. Our assessment identifies which is which.
  - q: "Do you use FAA-approved products?"
    a: |
      We use products approved by the original equipment manufacturer of the boots we are working on. OEM approval is the standard the aviation industry uses for boot conditioning. Product-specific approval varies by boot manufacturer.
  - q: "Can boot refurbishment be done outside?"
    a: |
      We strongly prefer to perform boot work indoors in a controlled environment to manage cure conditions and avoid airborne contamination. Outdoor work is possible in the right weather window but not preferred.
